THE CABLES BREAK THE CONTROL WHEREVER IT IS MEASURED

behav_all reported that applying dendritic cables, a neck and whole-level odour
together destroys the vision control. That was measured on health, and
behav_forage2 has since shown health cannot resolve the intact-minus-mirrored
contrast at sixty seeds in that arena at all -- on health every configuration
sits at p = 0.15 to 0.76. The conclusion had been read off a blind instrument.

Re-scoring the archived runs on collisions rescued the claim (behav_all/NOTE.txt)
but left one thing open: the striped arena compresses exactly this contrast, so
the null might have belonged to the room rather than to the cables. This run
settles that. Everything-applied on the FIXED forage arena, same seeds and
controls as behav_forage2, so the only difference from behav_all is the arena.

PRE-STATED in run.sh before it ran, with both outcomes named. Outcome 1 -- the
cables break the control wherever it is measured -- is what happened.

  intact vs mirrored, collisions per 1k tics, ties excluded

    plain      / striped        14.12 -> 20.12   +6.00   45 up / 15 down  p = 1.4e-04
    plain      / forage fixed   14.46 -> 20.67   +6.21   42 up / 16 down  p = 8.6e-04
    everything / striped        19.24 -> 18.03   -1.20   30 up / 28 down  p = 0.90
    everything / forage fixed   20.43 -> 19.38   -1.05   31 up / 28 down  p = 0.80

A clean two-by-two. The plain configuration is vision-dependent in BOTH arenas
and at almost identical effect size, +6.00 against +6.21 -- which is itself
worth noting, because nothing forced two arenas sharing no texture, geometry or
food appearance to agree to within 4%. The everything-applied configuration is
vision-blind in BOTH, and its sign counts are coin flips in both (30/28, 31/28).

The factor that decides the outcome is the configuration. The arena does not
touch it. So the null is a property of the cables, and behav_all's finding was
not arena-bound.

AND THE HEALTH ADVANTAGE WAS THE ARENA ALL ALONG.

    everything, health vs matched random
      striped        intact  +9.60   29/12   p = 0.012
                     mirrored +6.17   23/16   p = 0.337
      forage fixed   intact  +3.60   22/16   p = 0.418
                     mirrored -1.27   20/18   p = 0.871

behav_all's headline was that everything-applied scores slightly HIGHER than
plain while no longer needing its eyes, and that this was the disturbing part:
the effect changing its source rather than growing. In an arena that is not a
trap, the higher score is gone too -- +9.60 falls to +3.60 and stops being
significant. It was never the model being better. It was the striped arena
punishing its matched random agent harder than it punished this fly, and
behav_forage2 already showed why: remove the tall dark bar and the random
walker improves by more than four health.

So everything-applied is not "about as good and a worse experiment", which is
how behav_all put it. Measured in a fair room it is worse on the task, worse on
collisions (20.43 against plain's 14.46), and blind to its own retina. There is
nothing left to recommend it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW. The cables remain the largest single open-loop gain in
the project (T5 mirror-pair separation 0.005 -> 0.064, M17). Nothing here
touches that. What it shows is that the gain does not survive contact with a
closed loop, and that per-cell selectivity measured open loop is not a proxy
for a model that uses its eyes. Those two measures point opposite ways here,
and the closed loop is the one that answers the question the paper asks.

The frozen arm is deliberately not run: with --head 20 a frozen frame is not
frozen input, because the eyes keep turning across it. That control-validity
bug is recorded in behav_all/NOTE.txt and would make the arm meaningless.
