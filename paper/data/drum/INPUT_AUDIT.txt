Is the arena presenting the fly a stimulus its motion pathway can use?
Three dimensions, all measured in live gameplay, all adequate.

CONTRAST      RMS 55.6%, Michelson 99.4% (96.3% robust, 1st-99th pct).
              A laboratory optomotor drum runs at 50-100% Michelson, so the
              retina is not contrast-starved and repainting surfaces to differ
              from each other would add nothing.

SPEED         The drum response peaks sharply at 2 deg/tic = 70 deg/s, against
              the 62 deg/s predicted by 5 degree correlator arm separation and
              an 80 ms slow arm (one column swept per delay), with nothing at
              0, 4 or 8. Within 13% of prediction.

SPATIAL       On a single-elevation azimuth transect, peak spatial period
              25.6 deg, with 21.5% of power in 10-20 deg and 19.3% in 20-40,
              so 41% inside the correlator's working band. The grating tests
              used 15 deg.

              Note the retina oversamples: azimuth step 1.19 deg, Nyquist
              period 2.4 deg. The 5 degree figure used in the speed prediction
              is the correlator's ARM SEPARATION, set by the connectome, not
              the retinal sampling interval.

              (A first version of this measurement pooled +-10 degrees of
              elevation, which interleaves rows in the azimuth ordering and
              manufactured a spurious 3.6 degree peak. Single row only.)

CONCLUSION    The input is adequate on every axis we can measure. Combined
              with the drum result -- modulation is abolished by freezing the
              retina, peaks at the predicted speed, and does NOT reverse under
              a mirrored retina -- the model receives a usable motion stimulus,
              detects it, and fails to recover its direction. The failure is in
              the computation, which is where the shunt-cancellation result
              already placed it, and not in what the arena delivers.
