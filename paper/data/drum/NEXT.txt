Untested gaps, in the order they look worth doing.

1. SPATIAL frequency of the arena. We tested the temporal band twice and
   refuted it both ways, but a correlator is tuned in SPACE as well as time and
   that has never been checked. The grating tests use a 15 degree period; what
   Doom's wall textures subtend at the eye during play is unmeasured. If the
   scene's power sits mostly outside the correlator's spatial passband, no
   amount of contrast or speed adjustment helps. Measure the spatial power
   spectrum of retinal luminance during play against the m3k tuning, then, if
   it is off, build a WAD whose walls are striped at the preferred period.
   This is the version of the "dye the walls" idea that survives the contrast
   measurement: not more contrast, contrast at the right SIZE.

2. No efference copy. A real fly suppresses its own optomotor reflex during
   voluntary turns via corollary discharge, so it does not fight itself. This
   model has none: every turn it makes generates flow that drives a
   counter-turn. That is a negative feedback loop with no gate, and it may be
   why the closed-loop measures behave so differently from the open-loop ones.
   The drum experiment sidesteps it by imposing rotation the brain did not
   command, which is why it is the cleaner measurement.

3. Moving objects rather than self-motion. LC4 and LPLC2 are looming
   detectors and the arena can be populated with approaching objects at
   controlled speed, which tests a pathway that does not depend on the
   correlator at all.

4. Arena geometry. defend_the_center is a bare circular room, which is the
   closest installed scenario to a drum. A purpose-built cylinder with
   controlled wall period would be better and is a WAD away.
