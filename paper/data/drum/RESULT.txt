OPTOMOTOR DRUM, closed loop, defend_the_center (bare circular room)
5 seeds per drum speed, 350 tics, square wave reversing every 70 tics (4 s),
all-applied configuration with yaw decoded from DNp15.

  arm         slope        modulation by drum speed (deg/tic)
                             0       2       4       8
  intact    -0.052+-0.038  +0.23   +1.28   +0.18   +0.18
  mirrored  -0.073+-0.043  +0.25   +1.49   +0.53   +0.06
  blind     -0.004+-0.010  +0.05   +0.26   +0.04   +0.09

NO OPTOMOTOR RESPONSE. The intact slope is not significant (0.052 against
2 SE = 0.077) and mirroring the retina does not reverse it; the mirrored slope
has the same sign and a slightly larger magnitude. A directional counter-turn
would have to reverse under a mirrored retina, and it does not.

THE MODULATION IS NEVERTHELESS VISUAL. Freezing the retina drops the peak
from +1.28 to +0.26 and flattens every level, so what modulation exists
requires a live retinal image.

AND IT IS SPEED-TUNED, AT THE PREDICTED SPEED. The response peaks sharply at
2 deg/tic, which is 70 deg/s, against the 62 deg/s that 5 degree ommatidial
spacing and an 80 ms slow arm predict for one column swept per delay. Nothing
at 0, 4 or 8.

Read together: the model detects visual motion, at the speed its own wiring
predicts, and cannot recover its direction. That is what a projected DSI near
0.08 means, reached here through behaviour rather than through cell
recordings, and it is consistent with every other measurement in this project.

Caveat: 5 seeds per point. The peak at 2 deg/tic is large relative to the
other levels but the slope statistics are weak, and the non-monotonic profile
means a slope was the wrong summary for this data in the first place.
