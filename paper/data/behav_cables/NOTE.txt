THE CABLES ALONE

The last arm of the behav_all decomposition. Plain configuration plus
--dendrites 3 --g-axial 8 and nothing else, on behav_fixed's own 60 seeds and
controls, so the difference is the cables.

    against its matched random arm      health            tics
      plain          intact       +7.53 +- 6.66*   +34.00 +- 25.27*
      odour only     intact       +3.80 +- 6.73    +12.55 +- 26.98
      neck only      intact       +2.67 +- 7.02     -4.75 +- 25.53
      CABLES only    intact       +7.60 +- 6.53*   +26.52 +- 26.80
      all three      intact       +9.60 +- 7.06*   +28.85 +- 25.51*

THE COMPONENTS SPLIT CLEANLY. The cables are the only one of the three that
preserves the advantage over chance -- +7.60 against plain's +7.53, unchanged.
Odour and the neck each destroy it. Together, the cables supply the performance
and the other two supply a route to it that survives mirroring and freezing,
which is why behav_all scores well and fails its control.

AND THE JITTER IS THEIRS. Paired against plain on the same seeds:

    yaw chatter             +0.157 +- 0.015   WORSE
    collisions per 1k        +5.00 +- 2.94    WORSE
    tics between collisions -20.91 +- 13.66
    straightness            +0.101 +- 0.076   BETTER
    ground per unit walked   +0.975 +- 0.720  BETTER
    time railed at the clamp -0.0012 +- 0.0005 BETTER

behav_all's chatter was +0.149 and its collisions +5.11 for all three together.
The cables account for essentially all of both. I had attributed the jitter to
the neck, on the grounds that it was the only component adding a feedback loop
and that gaze loops oscillate. Plausible, and wrong.

What the cables buy is therefore mixed rather than bad: a fifth straighter
path and more ground covered, with a markedly jitterier turn command and a
third more collisions. Worth knowing before anyone turns them on by default --
they were measured open loop on grating selectivity, where none of this is
visible.
