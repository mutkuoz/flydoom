IS THE CENTRAL COMPLEX STARVED, OR BROKEN?

M20 localised this project's repeated failures to one junction: vision leaves
the optic lobe at 150-365 Hz and stops at the central complex, where ER fires
0.00 Hz, Delta7 0.00 and EPG 0.30. The obvious question is whether that is a
gain problem -- a circuit starved of drive by one uniform synaptic scale --
and the obvious test is to turn the drive up and sweep it.

WHAT THE CONNECTOME SAYS FIRST. ER receives excitation 24,903 against
inhibition 104,173, a ratio of 4.18 to 1, and almost all of that inhibition is
ER onto ER: the mutual inhibition that makes a ring attractor a ring attractor.
Its excitatory drive is TuBu, +14,560, the anterior optic tubercle relay. EPG
is 2.26 to 1 inhibited, and its largest single input is ER at -25,853.

That last number matters and it is the reason the first experiment failed.

EXPERIMENT 1: scale the excitatory input to ER, the visual gateway.

  gain      ER     EPG     PEN  Delta7     T4a     LC4   DNa02
     1    0.00    0.30    2.62    0.00  111.88  241.85  368.29
     4    7.83    0.03    2.73    0.00  113.85  242.64  369.17
    16   38.28    0.00    1.93    0.00  114.09  242.58  370.22
    64   92.69    0.00    0.88    0.00  113.69  240.82  369.01
   256  150.55    0.00    0.24    0.00  114.40  242.30  370.88

WAKING ER SILENCES THE COMPASS. ER goes from 0 to 150 Hz and EPG goes from 0.30
to exactly zero, PEN with it. Of course it does: ER is EPG's inhibition. The
ring neurons are what carve the non-preferred directions out of a bump, not
what drives it. Turning the visual gateway up 256-fold makes the compass
monotonically worse, and the rest of the brain does not move at all -- T4a,
LC4 and DNa02 are flat across the sweep, so the manipulation is well localised
and the result is not a side effect.

EXPERIMENT 2: scale the excitatory input to EPG and PEN, the recurrent loop
that has to sustain a bump.

  gain      ER     EPG     PEN  Delta7   heading r    null
     1    0.00    0.30    2.62    0.00
     4    8.79  336.34  383.26   50.03      -0.190  -0.171
    16    8.92  383.00  398.63   51.65      +0.283  +0.032
    64    8.94  397.83  399.13   51.88      +0.010  +0.001
   256    8.95  399.36  399.39   51.88      -0.010  -0.010

THE WHOLE CIRCUIT IGNITES AT ONCE. EPG, PEN, ER and Delta7 all wake together
from a single scalar on the loop, and the rest of the brain stays where it was.
So the silence is one recurrent loop failing to ignite rather than a diffuse
shortage of drive, which is a much more specific claim than M20 could make.

AND IT IS STILL NOT A COMPASS. At gain 16, run properly through M19 with its
controls -- five seeds, preferred headings fitted on half an episode and tested
on the other half:

    intact EPG    +0.086 +- 0.162    shifted null -0.063    384 Hz
    random optic  +0.331 +- 0.168                 -0.220    147 Hz
    random any    +0.269 +- 0.299                 -0.089     98 Hz
    frozen        +0.056 +- 0.114                 -0.048    384 Hz
    shuffled      +0.039 +- 0.229                 +0.102    375 Hz

EPG carries LESS heading information than 51 cells drawn at random. The
single-seed +0.283 that prompted this run was noise, which is what a result
appearing at one gain on one seed usually is.

The reason is visible in the rates: 384 Hz is the refractory ceiling. A ring
attractor is a SPATIAL pattern -- a bump somewhere and silence elsewhere -- and
a population firing flat out everywhere has no pattern to read. Note the bump
index reads 1.000 both here and in the original near-silent condition; it is
uninformative at both extremes and should not be quoted on its own.

CONCLUSION. The compass failure is structural, not a gain problem. Two
pathway manipulations, each swept over 256-fold, each well localised, and
neither produces a heading signal: driving the visual gateway silences the
compass because that gateway is inhibitory, and driving the recurrent loop
saturates it. What a ring attractor needs is a balance between recurrent
excitation and patterned inhibition, and a single scalar per pathway cannot
produce a balance -- which is, in miniature, exactly the per-cell-type gain
problem this project exists to report.

    python experiments/m21_ring.py --target ER  --gains 1 4 16 64 256
    python experiments/m21_ring.py --target EPG PEN --gains 1 4 16 64 256
    python experiments/m19_compass.py --seeds 5 --loop-gain 16
