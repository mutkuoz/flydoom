Each modality has its own descending pathway, and they are not the same one.

Odour on against odour off, all-applied visual configuration, single seed,
130 scored tics:

  readout        odour off   odour on     change in L-R
  DNa02 L-R        -27.89      -5.70          +22.19
  DNp15 L-R        -40.54     -39.79           +0.75
  DNp01 L-R        -72.70     -72.99           -0.29

Smell moves the steering asymmetry of DNa02 by 22 Hz and leaves DNp15
untouched. Vision is the other way round: the T4/T5-dependent directional
signal reaches DNp15 at 32 Hz while DNa02 draws 2.3% of its input from visual
populations and 0.34% from the horizontal system.

This matches the published characterisations rather than cutting against them.
DNa02 steers goal-directed walking under central-complex control, and
odour-guided navigation IS goal-directed steering. DNp15 (DNHS1) is the
optomotor neuron the horizontal system drives. The model has been reading one
descending pathway while delivering the other modality's stimulus.

Consequence for klinotaxis: it must be measured with the DNa02 readout, not
DNp15. Smell carries how close and never which way, since the antennae are
0.3 mm apart and receive identical drive, so an animal finds a source by
moving and comparing concentration against its own recent past, turning when
it falls. The existing run gives connectome r = +0.058 +- 0.117 against
shuffled r = -0.095 +- 0.074 on six episodes, which is the wrong sign and
under-powered, and it predates the corrected visual configuration.

VERIFIED ACROSS SEEDS (6 seeds, odour on minus odour off, change in L-R):

  DNa02   +20.62 +- 1.83   *   per seed: 22.2 20.1 20.1 21.4 16.7 23.2
  DNp15    -0.12 +- 1.12       per seed:  0.8 -1.6 -0.4  2.2 -0.5 -1.1
  DNp01    +0.55 +- 1.12       per seed: -0.3 -1.1  0.1  2.9  0.5  1.2

Every seed moves DNa02 in the same direction, and the effect is an order of
magnitude larger than its own between-seed spread. Neither of the other two
readouts moves at all. This is the largest and most consistent sensorimotor
coupling measured anywhere in this project, larger than any produced by
vision, and it sits on the descending neuron the literature assigns to
goal-directed steering, which is the pathway odour navigation should use.

Still to establish: whether it produces behaviour. A 21 Hz modulation of the
steering command is a coupling, not a turn toward an odour source, and the
klinotaxis measurement is the test.

KLINOTAXIS: NEGATIVE (18 episodes per arm, up from 6)

  connectome   r = +0.026 +- 0.062   7/18 episodes negative
  shuffled     r = -0.103 +- 0.050  15/18 episodes negative

The prediction was a negative r: smell falling should mean turn and search.
The intact brain shows no coupling; the degree-preserving shuffle shows the
predicted one significantly. A coupling the shuffle also produces is not from
the wiring, and this replicates the earlier six-episode run with better power.

So the 21 Hz modulation of the steering command does not become a turn toward
a source. That is now the pattern in every modality tested:

  vision  32 Hz directional signal at DNp15   no directional behaviour
  smell   21 Hz steering modulation at DNa02  no klinotaxis
  touch    3 Hz at DNa02                      not lateralised

In each case a sensory signal demonstrably reaches a descending neuron and
does not become behaviour. The model has sensorimotor COUPLINGS without
sensorimotor TRANSFORMATIONS.

The obvious candidate for what is missing is the structure that converts
descending commands into coordinated action, the ventral nerve cord, which
FAFB does not contain and no environmental change can supply.

PREDICTION RECORDED BEFORE READING THE SMELL ON/OFF BEHAVIOUR RUN

A 21 Hz shift in DNa02 L-R clears the 1.5 Hz deadzone and would command
21 * 0.44 = 9.2 deg/tic of yaw against a cap of 12, so on magnitude alone it
should dominate behaviour. But smell is on for the WHOLE episode, so the shift
is a sustained DC offset, and the yaw channel is baseline-centred with
tau = 3 s. A constant is precisely what that filter removes.

So the prediction is that smell on against smell off shows little or no
behavioural difference, for the same instrumental reason the drum would have
nulled before it was made to alternate, and NOT because the coupling is
absent.

The test that separates those: alternate the odour within an episode, as a
square wave well inside the filter's passband, and ask whether turning is
modulated in phase with it. That is the olfactory analogue of the alternating
drum, and if the coupling is real but DC-blocked it should appear there and
only there.

SMELL ON AGAINST SMELL OFF, 20 PAIRED SEEDS -- THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG

  tiles_visited   -7.20 +- 4.41  *
  yaw_abs_mean    -0.98 +- 0.27  *
  (healed, health_end, collisions, stuck, displacement, tics, spin: n.s.)

Odour DOES change behaviour: it reduces turn magnitude by about a third
(2.85 to 1.45 deg/tic on the first seeds) and reduces exploration. The
prediction recorded beforehand, that the sustained shift would be removed by
the tau = 3 s baseline filter and show nothing, is refuted. The DC of the
differential is removed, but odour also shrinks the MAGNITUDE of the
differential, and magnitude survives a high-pass.

THE SHUFFLE DOES NOT VALIDATE IT. The shuffled arm returns bit-identical
behaviour with odour on and off, which looks like a perfect control until the
yaw channel is examined: it takes only 7 distinct values across 20 seeds
against 20 distinct for the connectome, so it is quantised into a handful of
states and cannot register a shift of this size. It is uninformative here
rather than confirmatory. The random arm is matched to the connectome's own
command statistics per seed, so it inherits the effect by construction and is
not a control either.

The proper test is m13: gate the odour as a square wave and compare against a
bookkeeping arm that runs the identical schedule with the odour never enabled.

ALTERNATING ODOUR (m13), 10 seeds per arm, DNa02 readout

  odour   yaw(on) - yaw(off)  +0.504 +- 0.375  *
  none    yaw(on) - yaw(off)  -0.216 +- 0.827
  difference                  +0.719 deg/tic

The odour arm shows a phase-locked modulation of the steering command that
clears its own error bar; the bookkeeping arm, running the identical square
wave with the odour never enabled, does not. But the control's interval is
+-0.827, wider than the effect itself, so the DIFFERENCE between arms is not
established at this sample size even though the odour arm alone is
significant. Raised to 26 seeds, and adding DNp15 as a specificity readout:
odour lands on DNa02, so the visual readout should show nothing.

ALTERNATING ODOUR AT 26 SEEDS -- THE SPECIFICITY CONTROL IS FAILING

  DNa02 readout (where odour lands)   odour arm  +0.311 +- 0.237  *
  DNp15 readout (visual, control)     odour arm  +0.314 +- 0.123  *

The two are indistinguishable. Odour moves DNa02's left-right asymmetry by
21 Hz and DNp15's by 0.75, so at the RATE level the effect is specific to
DNa02; if the yaw modulation were driven by that asymmetry it would be far
weaker through DNp15, and it is not.

The likely explanation is a closed-loop confound: gating the odour changes the
trajectory, which changes what the eyes see, which perturbs every descending
readout whether or not odour reaches it. That is the same class of problem as
the shuffle-validity result already in the paper, where an arm that changes
behaviour changes its own stimulus.

The bookkeeping arms, identical square-wave schedule with the odour never
enabled, are what settle whether any of this is odour at all.

RETINA-FROZEN CONTROL: THE NEURAL EFFECT IS REAL, THE BEHAVIOURAL ONE IS NOT
ESTABLISHED

Odour on against odour off with the retina frozen on one frame, so both arms
receive identical visual input and the agent cannot have gone somewhere
different because it smelled something:

  DNa02   +17.32 +- 1.57  *   per seed: 15.0 17.3 17.0 20.8 16.2 17.6
  DNp15    -0.32 +- 0.58      per seed: -1.3 -1.0 -0.2 0.1 0.6 -0.0
  DNp01    -0.87 +- 1.29      per seed: -2.8 0.3 -1.5 -2.5 0.1 1.2

Against +20.62 +- 1.83 closed loop, so 84% of the effect survives removing
vision entirely, on every seed, and the specificity holds: the visual and
escape readouts do not move.

This separates two questions that the earlier measurements had merged.

  NEURAL      Odour specifically modulates the descending neuron the
              literature assigns to goal-directed steering, by about 17 Hz,
              independently of vision. Established.

  BEHAVIOURAL Whether that modulation produces a turn is NOT established. The
              m13 alternating-odour test shows the same phase-locked yaw
              modulation through DNa02 (+0.311 +- 0.237) and through DNp15
              (+0.314 +- 0.123), which cannot both be olfactory given the rate
              measurements above. Gating the odour changes the trajectory,
              which changes what the eyes see, which perturbs every readout.

The tension is structural rather than fixable. Holding vision constant makes
the measurement clean and removes the behaviour; letting the agent move
restores the behaviour and reintroduces the confound. It is the same result
the paper already records for shuffle controls, arriving again by a different
route: in closed loop, an arm that changes behaviour changes its own stimulus.
