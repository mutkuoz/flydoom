WHERE DOES THE ACTIVITY STOP?

Three circuits had been asked to do their job and could not, and each got its
own explanation: LC10a silent so fixation fails, LPLC2 silent so looming fails,
EPG silent so the compass fails. Three stories is worse than one measurement,
and the hypothesis was one sentence -- activity concentrates in the optic lobe
and never arrives at the deep populations that would do the computing.

Measured over one closed-loop episode in the configuration the behaviour tables
use, 22,800 substeps, every neuron, accumulated per SUBSTEP rather than per
Doom tic because sampling once a tic undercounts by up to 57 and would
manufacture the silence being tested for:

THE HYPOTHESIS IS WRONG, AND WHAT REPLACES IT IS BETTER.

Activity does NOT stop at the optic lobe. The visual projection neurons are the
most active population in the model -- 155 Hz on average, LC11 at 365, LC4 at
242 -- and they are exactly the deep visual cells the hypothesis said were
starved. Vision propagates out of the optic lobe perfectly well.

It stops at the CENTRAL COMPLEX, and it stops at one identifiable junction. ER,
the ring neurons that carry visual input into the ellipsoid body, fire at 0.00
Hz. Delta7, the inhibition that sharpens a heading bump, fires at 0.00 Hz. EPG
is 0.30 and PEN 2.62. The compass is not broken; IT IS NEVER DRIVEN. Nothing
crosses from a visual system running at 150-365 Hz into the ring neurons that
are supposed to anchor a heading to a landmark.

A CORRECTION I OWE. I have written in several places that LPLC2 is silent. In
the closed loop it fires at 207 Hz. The 0.00 Hz figure is from M4's synthetic
looming rig, under different drive, and generalising it to "LPLC2 is silent"
was wrong. The looming failure is that LPLC2 does not DISCRIMINATE expansion
from a size-matched static disc, not that it says nothing.

LC10a at 8.77 Hz is genuinely low -- thirty times quieter than LC4 and LC11 in
the same episode -- so M7's account survives, but as "quiet relative to its
neighbours" rather than "silent".

WHAT THIS IS WORTH. A claim about a junction is more useful than a claim about
three circuits: it explains the compass failure completely, it predicts that
anything requiring the central complex will fail the same way, and it names
where to look. The obvious suspect is the one the study already names -- the
per-cell-type gains the connectome does not specify -- but this localises the
question to the visual-projection-to-ring-neuron synapses rather than leaving
it spread across the brain.
