IS THERE A COMPASS?

Asked how a fly finds its way, the honest answer is that it mostly does not do
it by seeing food. It stabilises course on optic flow, moves in straight bouts
between saccades, avoids collisions by expansion, finds food by casting in
wind -- and it holds a direction with a COMPASS. The ellipsoid body carries a
ring attractor: a bump of EPG activity whose position encodes heading, updated
by self-motion and anchored to visual landmarks. Walking a fixed angle to that
bump is menotaxis, and it is how an insect crosses ground that offers no other
reference.

Every part is in this connectome and none had ever been driven or read: 51 EPG,
42 PEN, 20 PEG, 278 ER ring neurons carrying visual input, 42 Delta7.

Worth trying because this project's two big failures may not apply. Direction
selectivity and looming are computations on temporal ORDER, which this model
loses. A compass is a persistent STATE, which is a different thing to ask.

THE MEASUREMENT. Per-neuron EPG rates and true heading over a closed-loop
episode. Preferred headings are fitted on the FIRST half and the SECOND half is
decoded from them by population vector, so 51 directions are fitted on 350 tics
and tested on 350 it never saw.

THE CONTROL THIS NEEDED, and the first pass did not have. In a fixed arena
heading determines what is on the retina, so ANY visually driven cell
correlates with heading and decoding one proves nothing. The question is
whether EPG does better than an arbitrary population of the same size.

  arm            seeds   circ corr (held out)   shifted null   bump      Hz
  intact EPG         5      +0.129 +- 0.277        +0.211     0.994    0.44
  random optic       5      -0.117 +- 0.354        -0.111     1.000  148.72
  random any         5      +0.075 +- 0.286        +0.066     0.999   96.65
  frozen retina      5      +0.073 +- 0.080        -0.045     0.999    0.58
  shuffled graph     5      -0.138 +- 0.048        +0.075     1.000  158.02

NO. The intact decode, +0.129, is BELOW its own shifted null of +0.211 -- the
same decode against a deliberately mismatched heading trace does better. The
spread across seeds, +-0.277, swamps both. EPG carries no heading signal this
method can find, and it does not beat 51 cells picked at random.

WHY, AND IT IS THE SAME REASON AS EVERY OTHER SILENT POPULATION HERE. EPG fires
at 0.44 Hz. Random optic-lobe cells in the same episodes fire at 149 Hz and
random cells anywhere at 97 Hz. The bump index near 1.0 is not a ring
attractor; it is a near-silent population where one or two active cells make
the population vector trivially "sharp", and it should not be read as
structure.

THAT IS NOW THREE. LC10a is silent and fixation fails. LPLC2 is silent and
looming fails. EPG is silent and the compass fails. The pattern across them is
not about any one circuit: activity in this model concentrates in the optic
lobe, at 100-150 Hz, and does not arrive at the deep populations that would
have to do the computing. Whatever the reason -- and the per-cell-type gains
the connectome does not specify are the obvious suspect -- it is the same
reason three times, and it is a better description of the model's limit than
three separate circuit stories.
