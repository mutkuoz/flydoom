LOOMING, RE-SCORED AGAINST A CONTROL THAT IS ACTUALLY MATCHED

The paper's looming table compares the expanding disc against a STATIC disc
held at the sweep's FINAL size. That control is defective and the paper says
so: the sweep runs from 5 to 70 degrees, so holding the end point delivers far
more total drive than the stimulus it controls for. "Static beats looming" was
therefore never evidence of anything.

m4 has carried a matched control for a while without scoring on it. static_
matched holds the sweep's MEAN angular size for the whole window, so the two
stimuli deliver the same total drive and differ only in that one expands.
The acceptance check now uses it.

Intact connectome, l/v 40 ms, 1 s, driving L1+L2+L3, bias 0, mean rate in Hz:

  condition          L1      LC4   LPLC2    LC11   DNp01     MDN
  looming          61.8    26.42    0.00    6.55   131.50    5.25
  static          138.4    77.21    0.00   44.69   145.50    6.25
  static_matched   60.5    26.44    0.00    6.94   141.00    4.50
  receding         59.8    24.31    0.00    5.87   147.00    3.50
  blank            58.1    24.90    0.00    4.97   120.00    3.50

READING

1. LC4 gives 26.42 Hz to the looming sweep and 26.44 Hz to a static disc of the
   same mean size. To four significant figures the expansion adds NOTHING. The
   old comparison left room to argue that looming was present but swamped;
   this one does not. What LC4 reports is mean angular size.

1b. The shuffled arm says the same thing: 5.38 looming against 5.16 matched
   static, and 71.5 against 70.0 at DNp01. Whatever is being measured does not
   need the wiring.

2. The verdict is unchanged -- M4 still fails -- but two of the three controls
   fail for a sharper reason than before. Receding (24.31) is barely below
   looming (26.42), and blank is 24.90, which is 94% of the looming response.
   Nearly the whole "looming response" is present with no object at all.

3. DNp01, the giant fibre, sits at 120-147 Hz in EVERY condition including the
   blank. It is not reporting the stimulus, it is saturated, and no behaviour
   downstream of it in this model can mean anything. That is a stronger
   statement than "looming fails" and it belongs in the write-up.

4. LPLC2 is 0.00 Hz throughout, as in every previous run.

WHAT MAKES MDN BURST

MDN commands backward walking, and the closed-loop agent reads it as a reverse
command, so what drives it is a question about behaviour and not only about the
escape circuit. Two measurements, neither of them looming:

  visual (this table)  blank 3.50, matched static 4.50, looming 5.25, and the
                       large static disc 6.25. MDN tracks how much DARK is in
                       the field, in the same rank order as LC4, and the
                       expanding stimulus is not special.

  antennal contact (m11, 3 seeds x 200 tics, forced contact)
                       none 7.18, left 6.50, right 8.26 Hz, with BPN flat at
                       30.5-30.7. Contact moves MDN by about 1 Hz and not
                       consistently in one direction, so in this model
                       collision does NOT command a reversal.

So the model's reverse command is driven by dark area in view -- a nearby wall
fills the eye, MDN rises, the decoder subtracts it from the walking command.
That is at least the right kind of behaviour for the wrong reason: real MDN is
driven by visual projection neurons too, but backward walking in flies is
evoked by looming and by antennal contact, and here it is evoked by neither.
