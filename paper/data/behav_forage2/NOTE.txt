WALLS THE FLY CAN TELL FROM THE GROUND

behav_forage built an arena meant to be honest to a fly and got a strange
result: the intact model scored FIVE BELOW its matched random agent while the
mirrored one scored five above it, and that inversion was written up as "the
strongest vision-dependence evidence in the project -- only a genuine, signed
visual steering signal can flip a sign."

It was not. The arena was broken, and this run says so.

WHAT WAS BROKEN

Someone watched the video and said the walls and the floor look the same.
Measured, they were: contrast 0.126 against 0.122, the same dominant spatial
period, differing only by a brightness offset. A photoreceptor adapts the mean
away, so to the model there was no boundary between the surface it walked on
and the surface it walked into.

The d' of 1.86 quoted when that arena was built measured separability of MEAN
LUMINANCE between wall-looking and ground-looking lenses -- precisely the
quantity adaptation removes. It was the right statistic computed on the wrong
variable, and it certified an arena that carried no usable wall signal at all.

Re-measured in the units the network actually receives (drive after
adaptation), the three arenas separate wall from ground at:

    forage, broken walls    d' = 0.54
    fly arena, striped      d' = 0.59
    forage, walls fixed     d' = 1.01

THE FIX. Set the two textures to explicit and different contrasts rather than
to amplitudes that happened to collide: wall 0.22 against ground 0.08, a factor
of 2.7, with mean 174 against 106 and six times the local detail. Three cues,
two of which survive adaptation. Same seeds, same controls, same brain, same
everything else as behav_forage -- the arena is the only difference.

WHAT IT DID NOT DO

It did not make the model out-heal chance. Against its matched random agent,
intact health is +4.07 (21 up / 16 down of the 37 seeds that moved, p = 0.511).
And against the broken arena, paired seed by seed, the health difference is 23
up / 24 down, p = 1.0000 -- the absolute means rose (18.20 -> 21.93) but not
reliably on any given level.

The absolute recovery is real and is worth stating, because the fixed forage
arena is now as good a room for this model as the striped one, or better:

                        fly arena    forage broken    forage fixed
    health                 21.07          18.20           21.93
    survival (tics)        445.7          431.2           445.3
    collisions per 1k      14.12          16.67           14.46
    stuck fraction         0.343          0.311           0.242

But health does not separate the model from chance here, for a reason visible
in the baseline rather than the model: the RANDOM agent scores 13.53 in the
striped arena and 17.87 here. The forage arena is simply kinder to a random
walker -- no tall dark bar to be trapped against -- so the headroom between
chance and competence falls from 7.5 health to 4.1, and sixty seeds no longer
resolves it. The model did not get worse. The floor came up.

WHAT IT DID DO, AND THIS IS THE RESULT

Health was never the right benchmark. Mirroring the retina and asking whether
the model still moves well is, and it answers cleanly. Connectome against
itself, paired by seed, no random baseline involved:

    collisions per 1k tics, intact vs mirrored
      fly arena, striped       14.12 -> 20.12    45 up / 15 down   p = 0.000135
      forage, BROKEN walls     16.67 -> 16.96    30 up / 30 down   p = 1.000000
      forage, walls fixed      14.46 -> 20.67    42 up / 16 down   p = 0.000862
      pooled over the two readable arenas        87 up / 31 down   p = 2.5e-07

Reversing the retina left-to-right makes the model hit things about 43% more
often. It replicates across two arenas that share no texture, no geometry and
no food appearance, and in the arena where wall and ground were indistinguish-
able it does exactly nothing -- 30 up, 30 down, a dead heat to the seed.

That null is the control this project has wanted. The standing objection to
every mirroring result is that scrambling a retina is a lesion, and lesions
degrade things generally. Here the identical lesion in the identical brain on
the identical task costs nothing at all the moment the walls stop carrying
information. The effect is not disruption. It is the loss of a signal that was
being used.

Two further checks, both of which had to pass and did:

  - The random arm cannot tell the mirror is there (19.94 -> 19.46, p = 0.435
    striped; 19.21 -> 19.71, p = 0.791 forage-fixed), as it must not, since it
    never reads the retina. So the metric is not picking up something about the
    mirrored levels themselves.
  - free_run_tics gives p = 0.001 with the same 42/16 split, but it correlates
    with collisions at r = -0.871. It is the same measurement and is reported,
    not counted twice. Stuck fraction moves the same way independently,
    0.24 -> 0.30, 38 up / 22 down, p = 0.052.

WHAT THIS RETRACTS

behav_forage/NOTE.txt point 2 -- "mirroring no longer abolishes, it inverts...
the strongest vision-dependence evidence in the project" -- is withdrawn. The
inversion was an artifact of an arena in which the visual signal was noise. In
that situation using the signal hurts (intact 18.20, five below random's
23.20), and scrambling it helps (mirrored 21.67), which is what was observed
and what was over-read. Fix the walls and the arm ordering returns to what the
striped arena gives:

    against matched random      intact   frozen   mirrored
      fly arena, striped         +7.53    +4.47     -0.67
      forage, broken walls       -5.00    +2.67     +5.13    <- inverted
      forage, walls fixed        +4.07    +3.33     +1.87

The general lesson is the one that produced the bug in the first place: verify
an arena in the units the network receives, not in the units of the image file.
A cue the photoreceptors adapt away is not a cue.

THE REPLICATION, PRE-STATED AND THEN RUN

Before seeds 100-159 were run, the claim under test was written down: the model
collides less than its mirrored self in the fixed forage arena, tested by the
ties-excluded sign test on collisions_per_1k_tics. It replicates almost exactly.

    block 1, seeds  40- 99    intact 14.46   mirrored 20.67   42 up / 16 down   p = 8.6e-04
    block 2, seeds 100-159    intact 14.81   mirrored 20.04   42 up / 17 down   p = 1.6e-03
    both blocks, n = 120                                      84 up / 33 down   p = 2.7e-06

    grand pool with the striped arena, n = 180               129 up / 48 down   p = 9.3e-10

Worth noting against this project's own history: the health advantage in the
striped arena HALVED on replication, +7.53 on the first sixty seeds and +3.80 on
the next sixty, and we recorded that the first block had evidently been the
favourable half. The collision effect does nothing of the kind -- -6.21 then
-5.23, with the sign counts landing on 42/16 and 42/17. It is a more stable
measurement, not merely a more significant one.

At n = 120 the whole panel moves, including the two measures that had been flat:

    intact vs mirrored, fixed forage arena, n = 120
      collisions per 1k   14.64 -> 20.36    84 up /  33 down, 3 tied   p = 2.7e-06
      free-run tics       82.90 -> 61.76    84 up /  33 down, 3 tied   p = 2.7e-06
      stuck fraction       0.25 ->  0.30    75 up /  45 down           p = 7.9e-03
      health              21.80 -> 14.00    52 up /  31 down, 37 tied  p = 2.8e-02
      survival (tics)    445.07 -> 420.56   53 up /  36 down, 31 tied  p = 8.9e-02

BOTH CONTROLS PASS HERE, WHICH THEY DO NOT IN THE STRIPED ARENA.

The paper records that the frozen-retina arm "is not clean" and that the
dissociation therefore rests on the mirror control alone. That is true where it
was measured and false here. Scored on collisions, intact against each control:

    arena            control     intact    ctrl     gap     n   up / down        p
    fly, striped     mirrored     14.12   20.12   +6.00    60   45 / 15   1.4e-04
                     frozen       14.12   16.08   +1.96    60   36 / 23   0.117
    forage, fixed    mirrored     14.64   20.36   +5.72   120   84 / 33   2.7e-06
                     frozen       14.64   18.99   +4.36   120   81 / 38   1.0e-04

Freezing the retina on one frame costs the model 4.36 collisions per 1k tics in
this arena and is not distinguishable from noise in the striped one. So the
dissociation rests on both controls here, not on the mirror alone. Note this
frozen arm carries no --head, so the control-validity bug recorded in
behav_all/NOTE.txt -- where a movable head turns the eyes across a held frame
and feeds the no-vision arm self-generated motion -- does not apply to it.

On health the frozen arm still misbehaves, and in the familiar direction:
intact +5.04 (p = 0.115), mirrored +0.73 (p = 0.416), frozen +3.92 (p = 0.017).
The arm that should be worst is the only one significant. That is the same
quantisation problem as everywhere else in this file -- 41 of 120 seeds tie --
and it is why the collision table above is the one to read.

The health line retires a limitation recorded in the paper. Measured in the
striped arena, intact-minus-mirrored health was +3.50 +- 4.63 at n = 120 and we
calculated that roughly 210 seeds would be needed to resolve it, declining to
run toward that number. In this arena the same contrast is +7.80 at n = 120 and
already significant. The effect was twice as large here all along; the striped
arena's tall dark bar hurts the intact and mirrored models about equally, which
compresses exactly the difference the contrast is trying to measure.

    bash paper/data/behav_forage2/run.sh        # seeds  40- 99
    bash paper/data/behav_forage2/run_ext.sh    # seeds 100-159
