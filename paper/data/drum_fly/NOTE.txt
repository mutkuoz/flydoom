THE TETHERED DRUM IN AN ARENA BUILT FOR THE FLY

Arena: health_gathering_fly (scripts/build_fly_arena.py). Same map, three
changes, all of them environment:
  walls   vertical square-wave grating, period 128 map units = 18-37 degrees
          at the distances walls sit at, inside the 20-30 degree optimum
  floor   bright irregular ground, features 32-64 units, replacing a dark
          liquid flat
  light   flat and maximum, no fake depth shading
Interface: anatomical eye map, full field, corrected turn sign. Tethered:
commands recorded and discarded, no walking, 8 seeds per speed, 350 tics.

WHAT THE ARENA DID TO THE RETINA (open loop, same walk in both arenas)

                                    sky arena   fly arena
  frame-to-frame |change|, whole eye   0.0250      0.0466     1.9x
  frame-to-frame |change|, ventral     0.0069      0.0240     3.5x
  |contrast| median                    0.339       0.310
  ground raw luminance                 0.023       0.250

Frame-to-frame change is what a correlator integrates. Static contrast falls
slightly and that is the right trade: what it loses is the sky-against-ground
step, a large static edge no motion detector can use.

READING
A response appears where the sky arena had none: the drum now moves the
steering neuron by about 1 Hz and the command by about half a degree per tic,
against a frozen-retina arm that is exactly zero, so it is visually driven.
In the sky arena the same measurements were +0.39+-0.50 Hz and +0.06+-0.18.

It is not direction selective. The mirrored arm, which reverses every
horizontal optic-flow signal, gives the same sign and nearly the same size.
A real optomotor response must reverse; this one does not. So the arena buys
what it was built to buy -- roughly three times the visually driven signal at
the steering neuron -- and the barrier is unchanged: per-cell direction
selectivity capped near 0.08 where a fly needs ~0.5, which is a property of
the neuron model and the wiring, not of the world we show it.
