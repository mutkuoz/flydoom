IS HEADING REPRESENTED ANYWHERE?

M19 asked one population, because EPG is where a fly keeps its compass, and the
answer was no. That leaves the larger question unasked: is the compass simply
in the wrong place in this model, or is heading absent from the brain
altogether?

This asks all 434 cell types with at least 20 cells at once, with M19's
discipline on each: preferred headings fitted on the first half of an episode
and scored on the second, every type compared against RANDOM cells drawn in the
same number, and a rolled-heading null. The size-matched control is not
optional -- in a fixed arena heading determines what is on the retina, so any
visually driven cell correlates with heading, and without the control the
answer is "everything encodes heading", which is true and useless.

THE RANKED LIST LOOKS LIKE A FINDING AND IS NOT.

  type          cells   heading r   random   margin
  Tm8b            126      +0.489   -0.440   +0.929
  Tm5e            506      +0.666   -0.203   +0.869
  TmY11           377      +0.534   -0.260   +0.794
  ...
  EPG              47      +0.265   -0.268   +0.533   (rank 15 of 434)

Two things give it away. The random controls are systematically NEGATIVE, which
they should not be -- a random population ought to score near zero -- so the
margins are inflated by unlucky draws rather than by real signal. And 434 tests
were run, so the largest of them is impressive by construction.

THE TEST THAT SETTLES IT.

  margin across all 434 types   mean +0.036   sd 0.261   median +0.035
  largest observed margin       +0.929        3.4 sd above the mean
  expected largest of 434 draws +0.947
  types with positive margin    239 of 434

The biggest number in the list sits where the biggest of 434 noisy numbers is
expected to sit -- slightly BELOW it. Half the types are positive, which is
what a distribution centred on nothing gives. There is no outlier, so there is
no finding.

NO. Heading is not represented anywhere in this brain above chance. That is a
stronger statement than M19's and it is the one worth carrying: the compass is
not misplaced, the quantity is absent.

It also sharpens what M21 and M22 showed. M22 can produce a localised bump by
setting the excitation/inhibition ratio, and that bump encodes nothing; M23
says no other population is encoding it either. So the model is not computing
heading somewhere else and failing to route it to the ellipsoid body. Nothing
in it knows which way it is facing.

EPG at rank 15 of 434 is worth one sentence and no more. The 97th percentile
of 434 tests contains about 22 types by chance, so being in it is not
evidence. It is mentioned because leaving it out of a ranked list would be
selective, not because it means anything.

METHOD, worth keeping. With hundreds of simultaneous tests, the question is
never whether the top score is large -- it is whether the top score is larger
than the top score of that many draws from the same noise. mean + sd *
sqrt(2 ln n) is the quick form of that check and it took one line to refute
this whole table.
